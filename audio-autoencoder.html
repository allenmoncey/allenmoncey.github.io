Complex-valued autoencoder for machinery audio

Generates realistic CNC machine sound from complex STFT inputs for virtual factories and VR operator training.

Role
First author; model design, training, evaluation
When
2023 – 2025, ASU
Published
Manufacturing Letters 44 (2025)

The problem

Virtual factories and VR operator-training environments feel convincing only when the machines sound right. The sound of a cut carries real process information, and recorded clips cannot cover every combination of tool, material and cutting parameter, so the audio needs to be generated.

Approach

I designed a complex-valued convolutional autoencoder that works directly on the complex short-time Fourier transform (STFT) of machine audio. Keeping the real and imaginary parts together lets the network model magnitude and phase jointly, which is what audio needs to sound natural once it is converted back to a waveform.

  1. Convert CNC machining audio into complex STFT representations.
  2. Encode with complex-valued convolution layers into a compact latent representation.
  3. Decode back to a complex spectrogram and invert to audio.
  4. Evaluate reconstruction error and listen to generated audio for use in simulation.

Results

≈ 0.20reconstruction MSE
NAMRC 53presented; NSF travel award

The model reached a reconstruction MSE of about 0.20 and produced high-fidelity machining audio suitable for virtual-factory and VR training environments. The work was presented at NAMRC 53, where I received an NSF travel award.

My role

First author. I built the model, the data pipeline and the experiments, and wrote the paper with Dr. Binil Starly and Dr. Ashif Iquebal.
