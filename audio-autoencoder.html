Complex-valued autoencoder for machinery audio

Generates CNC machine sound from complex STFT inputs so virtual factories and VR training can sound like the real machine.

Role
First author; model design, training, evaluation
When
2023 – 2025, ASU
Published
Manufacturing Letters 44 (2025), NAMRC 53

The problem

Virtual factories already use cameras, thermal sensors and vibration data, but almost none use sound, even though machinists and monitoring systems rely on it to catch tool wear and spindle problems early. Generating realistic machine audio for each process condition would let a virtual factory or VR trainer sound like the real shop floor. Unlike speech and music, though, there is very little machinery audio to train on, so the model has to learn from a small dataset.

Approach

I designed a convolutional autoencoder that works on the complex short-time Fourier transform (STFT) of machine audio. The real and imaginary parts of the STFT go in as two channels, so the network learns magnitude and phase together and the output can be inverted straight back to a waveform.

  1. Convert a 0.74-second audio clip to its STFT and split it into real and imaginary components (257 × 128 × 2).
  2. Encode with 2D convolution layers and SiLU activations; decode with transposed convolutions.
  3. Split the output back into real and imaginary parts and apply the inverse STFT to get audio.
  4. Compare real and decoded audio in the time and frequency domains. A second decoder with residual connections extends a 0.74-second clip to 1.48 seconds.

Training used the 4-hour designed CNC audio dataset (see the dataset project) on an NVIDIA RTX A6000 workstation.

Results

The model reconstructed machine audio with a mean squared error of about 0.20, and the frequency spectra of decoded audio closely matched the real recordings. It is a first step toward real-time, condition-specific machine sound in virtual factories and VR operator training. I presented the work at NAMRC 53 with an NSF travel award.
