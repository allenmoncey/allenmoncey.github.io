The problem

Directed energy deposition (DED) is a form of metal 3D printing: a laser melts metal wire as a robot moves the print head, building a part layer by layer. Robotic DED can print and repair large metal parts, but only if the head, the robot and the positioner behave as one machine. In a research cell built from different vendors’ equipment, faults between them stop builds and take the cell out of service.

Approach

I run metal additive manufacturing research on a wire-laser DED cell:

  • Deposition head: Meltio wire-laser metal head.
  • Robot: FANUC R-1000iA/80F six-axis industrial robot.
  • Positioner: 2-axis (tilt and rotate) servo positioner for part orientation.

When builds failed, I traced the faults to the integration between the Meltio software and the robot controller, isolated the cause and fixed it, so the cell could keep running experiments.
