Vision inspection of bandsaw teeth with M.K. Morse

Machine-vision inspection that extracts each tooth’s front-edge profile and flags defective teeth on a Hotelling T² control chart. Presented at NAMRC 53 with M.K. Morse.

Role
Vision pipeline and monitoring method
Partner
The M.K. Morse Company
Presented
NAMRC 53 (2025)

The problem

Bandsaw teeth are milled before they are set and heat-treated. Band sawing removes less than 0.001 in (0.025 mm) per tooth, so the condition of each tooth edge matters: burrs and deformation left by milling cause uneven wear, premature blade failure and out-of-square cuts. They also confuse the machine vision used in the next step, setting, which cannot tell a burr from the tooth itself. Tooth quality was judged by eye, so M.K. Morse wanted a quantitative measure of each tooth.

Approach

  • Imaging: a 16 MP monochrome camera with a telecentric lens, with a bar light and a back light, viewing the band from above before setting.
  • Experiment: milling runs varying feed, burr-plate material and burr-plate indexing, sampling the first, middle and last band in each pack.
  1. Segment the teeth with contour detection in OpenCV, chosen over Canny edges, Otsu thresholding and active contours because the backlit images segment cleanly and contours give precise profiles.
  2. Crop each tooth, remove its orientation and extract the front-edge profile.
  3. Describe the profile with the mean, standard deviation and kurtosis of its edge coordinates, then standardize them.
  4. Build a Hotelling T² control chart from eight teeth that experts judged good (α = 0.1) and flag any tooth above the upper control limit.

Results

Across teeth milled with different parameters, good teeth scored below the limit (for example 7.6, 9.5 and 15.7) while burred or deformed teeth scored far above it (240, 342 and 739). The method separated the good teeth from the bad ones in the experiment and turned a visual judgment into a single number that can be tracked on a control chart. Next steps are a broader experiment, faster automated code and profile skewness as a feature for spotting burrs and chips.
