Vision inspection of bandsaw teeth with M.K. Morse

Camera-based inspection that measures each saw tooth’s front edge and flags defective teeth on a statistical control chart. Built with M.K. Morse; presented at NAMRC 53.

Problem
Burrs left when bandsaw teeth are milled cause early blade failure, and teeth were judged by eye.
What I built
Camera-based inspection that measures each tooth’s front edge and scores it on a statistical control chart.
Result
Across 38 teeth, good teeth scored 7.6–15.7 and defective ones 240–739, against a limit of 17.1.
Why it matters
Turns a visual judgment into one number a plant can track. Built with The M.K. Morse Company and presented at NAMRC 53.
Role
Vision pipeline and monitoring method
Partner

The problem

Bandsaw teeth are milled, then set and heat-treated. Each tooth removes less than 0.001 in (0.025 mm) of metal per cut, so a small burr or deformed edge matters: it causes uneven wear, early blade failure and crooked cuts.

Burrs also confuse the vision system at the next step, setting, which can’t tell a burr from the tooth. Teeth were judged by eye, so M.K. Morse wanted a measurable score for each one.

Approach

  • Imaging: a 16 MP monochrome camera with a telecentric (distortion-free) lens, a bar light and a back light, viewing the band from above.
  • Experiment: milling runs varying feed, burr-plate material and burr-plate indexing, sampling the first, middle and last band in each pack.
  1. Find each tooth’s outline with OpenCV contour detection, which worked best on the backlit images.
  2. Crop and straighten each tooth and extract its front edge.
  3. Measure the edge: its average position, how much it varies, and how spiky it is (kurtosis).
  4. Combine the measures into one score per tooth on a Hotelling T² control chart, using eight teeth experts judged good as the baseline. Teeth scoring above the limit are flagged.

Results

38 teethinspected from 20 high-resolution images
17.1control limit: teeth scoring above it are flagged

Good teeth scored below the limit (for example 7.6, 9.5 and 15.7); burred or deformed teeth scored far above it (240, 342 and 739). Next steps are a broader experiment, faster automated code and a new edge measure for spotting burrs and chips.
